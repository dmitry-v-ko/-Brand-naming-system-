# Speculative-fiction pattern research recipe

Type: universe research recipe, not an independent generation-method count. Editorial implementation; actual source L03 is recorded in [lexical evidence](../docs/research/LEXICAL_ACCESS_EVIDENCE.md).

## When and what to collect

Use for future nostalgia, imagined instruments and technology with human qualities. Avoid if recognizable franchise vocabulary dominates or if the product needs literal scientific claims. Required: brand promise, acceptable strangeness, and selected existing operator. Default: two reference entries, two general patterns, three unrelated original constructions. An accessible secondary entry is enough to study a pattern, not enough to claim direct reading of its cited novel.

## Retrieval and extraction

1. Search `site.sf-encyclopedia.com communication device` or an exact observed term. Open the entry. Record author/work/date only as the source supports them.
2. Separate term → described function → observed formation relationship → source certainty. Do not reverse-engineer exact historical letter deletions when only a broad relationship is documented.
3. Extract an abstract grammar: e.g. ordinary-language compression, or speculative modifier + familiar technical head. Compare two examples before calling it a general corpus tendency; with one entry call it a local pattern observation.
4. Put original coined terms in the evidence ledger, not the candidate pool. Populate the grammar with unrelated brief vocabulary. Use `fragment-blending`, `prefix-attachment` or `intact-word-compounding` with explicit operations.
5. Assess whether new material works without knowing the fictional source. Park if all candidates merely echo a recognizable artifact.
