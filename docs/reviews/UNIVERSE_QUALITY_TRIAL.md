# Universe deepening and bounded adaptation trial

Date: 2026-11-11. Baseline repository commit: `7d11f2b`. Scope: parallel universe assignments, bounded adaptation, and form/context assessment before origin stories. This is a small live execution on an owner-supplied private brief, not a quality benchmark or consumer study. Actual names, source records, worker references and session state remain outside the public library.

## Execution

Three fresh-context workers received the same frozen brief r1 and lexical-research procedure, with three distinct universe assignments. Each worker researched two source anchors and applied the existing clipping operator once per anchor, producing three variants per anchor. Each wrote a separate result; the conductor alone reconciled shared records. All three returned full assignment/method/universe/run/revision identities; all three assignments completed. This is one method across three worlds, not three methods or an all-eighteen sweep.

Baseline: 24 unique candidate records, comprising six unchanged controls and eighteen adaptations. All 24 were mechanically conforming; worker assessments were 2 retained, 16 parked and 6 rejected. Zero improvements in one universe was accepted without manufacturing a representative. The conductor retained one provisional research lead and parked the other 23; user favorites remained empty. Exact clipping maps were checked locally. No market/registry or native-speaker claims follow from these counts.

Workers recorded name-only predictions and identical customer-language contexts before appending source stories. They already knew their sources: this demonstrates record order, not blindness. A separate fresh evaluator received four forms, the brief and shared contexts without sources or etymologies; it read no session files and performed no browsing. Its editorial preference supported investigating the conductor's lead but is not audience evidence. The generators' prior judgments remain preserved.

## Findings and repairs

- Repeated method IDs need assignment identity. The contract and template now use full assignment tuples for acceptance, coverage and retry; a universe mismatch cannot close an assignment.
- The initial adaptation stop wording could end a pass on its first failed variant. Independent review caught this; the final rule evaluates the entire generated bounded batch, then closes the pass regardless of yield.
- A short adaptation can lose the useful image entirely. The final rule freezes required versus optional source recognition and its user/agent basis before generation, then records resulting edits/evidence after generation. The baseline did not yet contain this requirement; it is not retroactively marked compliant.
- A separate r2 diagnostic froze required source recognition as an agent test assumption, not a user preference. It generated two further clipping variants from one previously verified anchor. Both were mechanically conforming and parked; attractive brevity did not override lost required recognition. Baseline files/hashes and earlier candidate decisions remained unchanged. This targeted diagnostic is separate from the 24-record baseline, not a full rerun.

An independent reviewer approved the changed contract after the stop correction and manually examined three synthetic cases: wrong universe despite matching method stays unresolved; a failed first variant does not skip an improved second one; lost required recognition cannot be rescued by an attractive story. These are manual contract checks, not software enforcement.

## Limits

All three live workers chose clipping; other adaptation operators and all-eighteen execution were not rerun. Source translations and proposed readings retain their worker-recorded uncertainty. No claims of legal availability, commercial performance, improved conversion or automatic enforcement are made. Host-independent execution and real audience response remain untested. Existing method maturity remains draft. No screening redesign, validator, or new generation method was added.
