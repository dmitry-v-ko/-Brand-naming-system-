## Mechanism and boundary

Choose one unchanged lexical form with two independently supported senses relevant to the brief. Artifact: a two-sense intersection table. Unlike `metaphor-mining`, the second sense must already be lexical; unlike punning, there are not two different similar-sounding forms.

## Use, avoid and inputs

Use when two facets of an offer can meet in one compact real word. Avoid when one reading needs a convoluted explanation, regional spelling breaks the identity, or the only salient sense is unfavorable.

Required: two distinct facets, audience dialect and exclusions. Default: three candidate words, two dictionary senses each; one verified word is sufficient to demonstrate a narrow result, not to claim a broad lexical survey. Optional: dominant reading and tolerance for ambiguity.

## Procedure

1. Separate the brief into facet A and facet B. Propose three intersection leads, labeling unverified senses.
2. Open a dictionary entry for each promising lead. Capture two specific sense locators and regional/register limitations. A thesaurus association is not a second meaning.
3. Write two plain sentences using the identical form, each activating one documented sense. Then write one identical product introduction; note which reading may dominate.
4. Reject candidates whose second meaning is invented by the agent or conflicts with an exclusion. Keep alternative verified senses if relevant, but do not add a sense merely to rescue a weak name.
5. Return a small direct-word cluster. Any added adjective or ending creates a declared construction branch; it does not count as another lexical intersection.

## Selection and recovery

Select supported dual relevance, effortless reading and tolerable ambiguity. A verified negative sense can still make a poor name. Recover by changing which facet is sought or moving to another word; do not omit an unfavorable common reading from the table. If only one sense is verified, park the lead or use metaphor explicitly rather than calling it double meaning.

## Ordered combinations

`lexical-research` → `double-meaning` supplies candidate senses. `double-meaning` → `intact-word-compounding` adds an intact modifier while preserving the dual-meaning seed. `double-meaning` → `clipping` often destroys recognition; retain the original comparison and reject the clip if either reading vanishes.
