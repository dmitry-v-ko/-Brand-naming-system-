# Acceptable name occupancy

The goal is an acceptable risk, not a word unused by anyone anywhere. A search hit, taken domain or existing trademark alone does not exclude a name. This policy owns the user's business boundary and the four decision statuses; registry coverage remains separate.

## Ask the user directly

At screening intake, ask in the conversation language unless an explicit choice for this scope is already recorded:

> What level of existing use is acceptable: **soft** — coexistence with unrelated businesses; **balanced** — an independent brand without frequent confusion (recommended for the final shortlist); or **strict** — high uniqueness in relevant markets and search? Which should I use?

Give these short explanations with the question; do not ask the user to interpret trademark classes or legal risk. An explicit answer such as “use the coexistence mode” can establish soft mode; record the user's words and the selected mode. Accepting an alternative domain or an unrelated business alone does not identify a mode, since several modes may permit them. Clarify genuinely ambiguous answers. Balanced is the recommended starting option for a final shortlist, not a silently selected default. A tenant preference can prefill the proposal but does not substitute for the user's explicit selection. Reuse a recorded selection without asking again.

While waiting, continue authorized factual research and report findings/gaps. Do not issue a mode-dependent keep/reject decision. Record `mode: null`; use **Clarify** for the missing choice, or **Not checked** if evidence is also insufficient. For strict mode, also establish the priority markets, main/adjacent categories and required key domains plus an acceptable acquisition path; unknown constraints remain gaps. This authorizes no purchase or third-party contact.

## Three independent aspects

- **Rights:** similarity, actual goods/services, territories, status and priority of rights. The same Nice class does not prove conflict; different classes do not guarantee its absence. A softer business boundary cannot waive a rights concern.
- **Market distinguishability:** can the audience distinguish and find the product without mistaking it for part of another business?
- **Practicality:** workable domain strategy, pronunciation, spelling and language associations.

Assess each independently with evidence and unknowns; do not collapse them into a legal yes/no score.

## 1. Soft — acceptable coexistence

Useful for broad exploration and early shortlists. Keep a name when matches concern clearly different products, audiences or territories and there are no substantial signs of conflict or confusion so far.

Acceptable in principle: same-name businesses in other industries; a taken `.com` with a workable alternative address; a dictionary word, personal name or common metaphor; expired registrations, with current use checked separately. These are not automatic clearance: territorial reach, reputation and actual use may still matter.

An identical or close name in an adjacent field triggers further investigation, not automatic exclusion.

## 2. Balanced — an independent brand

Recommended for a final shortlist. The name should support a recognizable brand without repeatedly explaining “we are not that service”. Keep it when no substantial unresolved rights conflict is found in target markets, no strong same-name/close brand is likely to confuse the audience, a working domain and intelligible spelling are available, and language associations suit the product.

Do not recommend it when a confirmed close brand serves comparable goods, services or user tasks, or already occupies the recognition space the product needs. Explain the actual audience/market relationship: a distant, unrelated match is not enough. A local same-name laundry warrants checking details; an identical major software product is a much stronger obstacle for a new app.

## 3. Strict — high uniqueness

Useful for international expansion, strong search distinguishability and minimal explanation cost. In addition to balanced requirements:

- No notable identical or phonetically close brands in the main and adjacent categories in priority markets.
- Search results do not persistently belong to another known entity.
- Predefined key domains are available or have a realistic acquisition path.
- The name does not require recurring correction of pronunciation, spelling or unwanted associations.

This is a stricter **business filter**, not a legal requirement or promise of legal safety. Even strict mode does not require zero matches worldwide. An uninvestigated domain/search condition is an unknown, not a confirmed failure.

## Findings and decisions

For **each finding** report: **what was found → relevance to our product → what is confirmed → what is unknown → effect on the decision in the selected mode**. Separate rights, market and practical implications. Add one next action with owner and completion condition where clarification is needed. An aggregate lead is not a confirmed primary record.

Use exactly these final statuses, translated into the conversation language (Russian labels shown for consistency):

| Stored status | User label | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `continue` | Continue / Продолжать | No substantial obstacle found in the documented checked scope. State that scope; not launch permission. |
| `clarify` | Clarify / Уточнить | A specific uncertainty affects the decision; state the next step. |
| `do_not_recommend` | Do not recommend / Не рекомендовать | A confirmed basis is material under the selected mode; identify it and the affected aspect. |
| `not_checked` | Not checked / Не проверено | Evidence is insufficient. Neither approval nor rejection. |

If coverage is incomplete, use Clarify when a concrete uncertainty/action is identified, otherwise Not checked. A confirmed material basis can support Do not recommend before every remaining check is complete; keep those gaps visible. A potential-conflict flag or raw hit count alone never selects that status. Continue requires completion of the declared scope and no substantial unresolved obstacle; passing a registry query alone cannot support the overall decision. An individual unrelated finding can be acceptable while the overall check remains incomplete.

Never call a name “legally free”. Explicitly list unchecked registries, territories, spelling variants and other required work. Final rights assessment may need a qualified specialist. Changing the boundary or its domain/market constraints starts a new scope revision and invalidates the old recommendation for that scope; preserve dated evidence and reassess against the new criteria rather than silently re-dating or discarding it.
