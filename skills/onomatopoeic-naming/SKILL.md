- family: sound construction
- status: draft

- acceptance evidence: mechanical self-review only; independent walkthrough pending

## Operator and nearest neighbors

Turn a specified sound event into a proposed spoken imitation, then give that imitation a readable English-facing spelling. The indispensable input is an event: an actual recording that can be inspected, a reported noise, or an explicitly imagined noise. The transformation must retain an audible feature hypothesis such as a short impact, sustained friction, or a fading resonance. An arbitrary attractive syllable is `phonetic-invention`, even when retrospectively called a sound. A word selected for its associated activity is metonymic selection unless its sound is deliberately imitated here.

SIL's onomatopoeia definition supports resemblance between word sound and event/producer; SO01 in the [evidence note](../../docs/research/SOUND_ORTHOGRAPHY_EVIDENCE.md) records the inspected definition. Feature mapping and spelling choices below are editorial experiments. There is no universal spelling for a noise and no warranted inference that a hiss makes every audience feel calm.

## Use, avoid and inputs

Use for products with a characteristic action, sensory experiences, or a name whose spoken texture can carry an event cue. Avoid when no relevant event can be named, an imitation would trivialize a solemn offer, or silence is central and literal noises would contradict the brief. Do not invent claims about what a real product sounds like.

Required: offer, audience, tone exclusions and one event description with evidence type. If absent, propose two or three explicitly imagined events and proceed on an agent provisional branch for a sufficiently specified brief; do not invent user delegation. Default to one or two syllables, one event per candidate and ordinary Latin letters. Optional controls: sound duration, repetition, permissible consonant clusters, referent visibility and desired distance from familiar sound words. Default pronunciation target is a stated broad English proposal, not a global standard.

## Reproducible procedure

1. Create event cards E1–E3: producer/action, evidence type, exact received description, and salient onset/body/tail. Keep supplied descriptions separate from the agent's interpretation. If listening capability is absent, say so; a filename or transcript does not establish audible texture.
2. Translate each card into features: abrupt or gradual onset; transient or sustained body; damped or resonant tail; single or repeated rhythm. Mark these as reported, observed, or imagined. Do not fabricate measured frequencies or timings.
3. For each card propose two phoneme strings. Write the proposed mapping explicitly, for example abrupt onset→/t/, fading resonance→/ɪŋ/. This is an imitation choice, not a physical equivalence.
4. Spell each string, retaining an alternate spelling only as an orthographic variant of the same spoken proposal. Record added/deleted letters if adjusting it. Keep three event families rather than six variants of one impact.
5. Compare two or three presented names in the same offer sentence. Ask which event relationship and reading the user wants to retain. Readable imitation, fit to the actual offer, and distance between families take priority over decorative length.
6. Refine only the selected feature: damp the tail, shorten the sustain, or change one onset. If a repair abandons the event, open a `phonetic-invention` child instead of relabeling it onomatopoeia.

Private default: six raw proposals, three presented contrasts. Pause when two repairs lose event recognizability, all candidates converge on one familiar noise, or an essential recording cannot be inspected. Offer another imagined/reported event, a different method, or later audio review; never auto-export.

## Provenance and candidate contract

Follow the [source contract](../../docs/SOURCES_AND_RESEARCH.md) and [session protocol](../../docs/SESSION_PROTOCOL.md). Every candidate needs stable branch/candidate ID, spelling, proposed sound/stress, event ID/evidence type, exact feature→phoneme→letter steps, form, lineage evidence, session source IDs or `none`, intended association and main drawback. A constructed imitation is `invented`; supplied imagined descriptions use `not applicable`. An unchanged dictionary sound word is `attested` only after its form is actually inspected, with the supported sense cited. A verified dictionary entry does not verify the product's actual noise or user reaction.

For factual lexical claims, search the sound term plus reputable dictionary, open the sense, and record access date/locator. After two failed routes use clearly invented imitations or label the asserted lexical claim `unverified`. Bibliography SO01 supports the category, not the fictional candidate lineage. Keep real session evidence outside this checkout. Clearance is outside scope.

## Ordered combinations

`onomatopoeic-naming` → `respelling` freezes the spoken imitation and changes only its letters; C03→C07 illustrates that order. `onomatopoeic-naming` → `reduplication` repeats a chosen event unit with a declared rhythm; retain the original event and new echo map. `fragment-blending` → this method needs a fresh event link; a blend's incidental sound alone is insufficient.
