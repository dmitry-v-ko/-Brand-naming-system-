# Reduplication

- id: `reduplication`
- family: sound construction
- status: draft
- implementation: complete editorial procedure; independent acceptance pending
- requires: research-required for factual claims
- outputs: base-unit cards, echo grid, candidate ledger, feedback/state delta
- acceptance evidence: mechanical self-review only; independent walkthrough pending

## Operator and nearest neighbors

Make a second unit by copying a declared base, either exactly or with one controlled vowel/onset substitution, then join base and echo. The second unit must be derivable from the first through the recorded template. Two independently chosen words that happen to begin alike are alliteration; two meaningful intact words independently selected for their meanings are compounding. Neither becomes reduplication merely because the result sounds rhythmic. Prebuilt unrelated syllables are `syllable-recombination`; creating a base from individual phonemes first is `phonetic-invention` followed by this method.

The linguistic category includes repetition of roots, stems or parts: SO02 in the [evidence note](../../docs/research/SOUND_ORTHOGRAPHY_EVIDENCE.md). That factual definition does not establish a grammatical function for invented English brands. The exact/vowel/onset echo templates here are editorial creative operations. Do not claim these coinages form plurals, diminutives or intensifiers by a rule borrowed from another language. Brandpa's word-doubling advice provides limited practitioner context (SO04), not effectiveness evidence.

## Use, avoid and inputs

Use for a rhythmic identity, a playful or conversational offer, or a brief that wants a recognizable repeated signature. Adult products can explore the technique; babyishness is a possible reaction to ask about, not a universal property. Avoid when the user rejects repetition, needs a single compact beat, or demands immediate semantic precision from two separate source concepts.

Required: offer, acceptable playfulness, base unit or permission to propose bases, and pronunciation audience. Default: three invented monosyllabic bases, two units per name, stronger stress on the first, no semantic claims and no three-unit chains. Optional: meaningful supplied base, maximum length, vowel order, fixed coda, exact versus echo preference, and joined/hyphenated display. Hyphenation is a spelling control, not a new method.

## Reproducible procedure

1. Write base cards with ID, phonemes, spelling, syllable boundaries and any supported meaning. Label invented units meaning-free. For a multisyllabic base specify whether the whole form or one syllable is copied; never slide between those units.
2. Declare three templates before generating: exact `A+A`; vowel echo `CVC + CV′C`, with consonants fixed; onset echo `CVC + C′VC`, with vowel/coda fixed. For CV bases omit the coda explicitly. Select concrete substitutions, not “make it catchy.”
3. Fill a three-base × three-template grid privately. Record copied slots, changed slots and concatenation. Reject undeclared added sounds, an unrelated second unit or a changed stress/syllable count hidden in the spelling.
4. Ordinarily present two or three contrasts across at least two bases and all viable templates. Do not show nine near-identical strings if three suffice. Give each intended reading and a concrete drawback, such as segment ambiguity or an unwanted familiar phrase resemblance.
5. Compare exact repetition against a single echo change using the same base. Ask which rhythmic property to preserve. For refinement freeze the selected base and change only echo type, vowel, or onset in a fresh row.
6. If repetition is the objection, park this method and offer another operator. If the join is awkward, replace the base or narrow the echo substitution. Adding a smoothing vowel is a separate phonetic edit with its own step.

Private default: nine grid cells, three to six presented candidates. Pause after two unsuccessful repairs or when all alternatives repeat the same onset/vowel contrast. Invite deeper base exploration, another echo template, combinations, revisit, or finish. Batch completion never authorizes saving or export.

## Assessment and provenance

First check literal template conformance. Then consider readable segmentation, controllable stress, variety and the user's actual tone response. Do not assert that a particular vowel order is compulsory in all English, or that repetition guarantees recall. A syntactically valid echo may still be unsuitable for the brief.

Use the [source contract](../../docs/SOURCES_AND_RESEARCH.md) and [session protocol](../../docs/SESSION_PROTOCOL.md). Every record includes stable IDs, English spelling, proposed pronunciation/stress, base ID, unit boundary, exact copy/substitution, form, evidence, session sources or `none`, intended association and drawback. Invented base+echo is `invented / not applicable`. A verified real base supports its own sense only; the assembled name remains invented. If a grammatical or historical claim matters, open a linguistic reference for that exact claim and retain date/locator. After two failed routes omit the claim or label it unverified; continue with meaning-free bases. Never invent an etymology for an echo.

## Ordered combinations

`phonetic-invention` → `reduplication` creates a base with its phoneme ledger before copying. `onomatopoeic-naming` → `reduplication` repeats a recorded sound imitation; do not erase its event provenance. `reduplication` → `respelling` changes letters while freezing the two-unit pronunciation and echo map. A switch to `intact-word-compounding` opens a new branch when independent meaningful words replace copied units.
