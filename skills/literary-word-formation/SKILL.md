## Primary operator

Observe how an author makes a word perform a particular job, abstract a testable construction rule, then apply that rule to unrelated new material. The decisive artifact is the **pattern card between reading and generation**. Finding a striking fictional name and changing two letters does not instantiate this procedure.

The process works with drama, comedy, poetry, nonsense, speculative fiction or other prose. Genre supplies material; it does not define a different naming method. Science fiction remains a source universe. `fragment-blending`, `prefix-attachment` and other construction skills implement particular transferred patterns; they need no literary observation when used independently. This skill is useful when the reading changes which mechanism is chosen, what it must achieve and which output is rejected.

Use for a voice that needs a surprising yet interpretable construction, or when a literary passage exposes a productive tension such as two attributes compressed into one adjective. Avoid when the request depends on copying a recognizable character/place/artifact name, when the agent cannot inspect the passage, or when the desired seriousness cannot tolerate the observed register. Do not assume a satirical narrator or fictional speaker states the author's beliefs.

Required inputs: brand behavior, desired register and excluded associations. Defaults: two short passages with contrasting mechanisms, two ordinary ingredient pairs per chosen card, three to six candidate comparisons. Optional: supplied work, genre, target rhythm, structural complexity and distance from the source. If the user delegates selection, pick an accessible passage and record a provisional choice.

## Analysis and generation procedure

1. Offer contrasting mechanism previews rather than famous words: “compress two useful attributes,” “turn a state into an action,” or “build a transparent miniature description.” Give small invented examples with provisional labels; do not assert literary origins before inspection.
2. Open the primary text or a clearly identified edition. Record author, work, chapter/act/scene, precise line or paragraph locator, URL, access date and whether commentary was available. Search hits and lists of alleged author coinages are leads. An occurrence proves use in this edition, not invention, priority or a private compositional process.
3. Read enough surrounding context to identify word class, local function, voice and adverse implications. Separate three columns: **visible occurrence**, **text's explicit explanation**, **agent's analysis**. When no explanation exists, label segmentation and intended effect as hypotheses; include an alternative parse when material uncertainty affects the rule.
4. Write a pattern card with input types, retained structure, transformation, semantic job, variable slots and exclusions. Test it against the observed example and one negative case. A rule that merely says “sound fantastical” is not operational. If the rule cannot be specified, park the source and inspect another passage.
5. Remove source-specific words, proper names, plot objects and distinctive strings from the ingredient bank. Build new inputs from the brief or independently researched vocabulary; mark supplied ordinary ingredients as such. Preserve only a general construction and a chosen functional relationship. No author endorsement or newly coined “literary language” is implied.
6. Instantiate at least two different semantic tensions, not six respellings of the source token. Map every retained/deleted/added unit. If realization uses an existing construction skill, record the order: `literary-word-formation` → that skill. Construction validity and usefulness are separate judgments.
7. Compare candidates in a plain introduction without literary explanation. Assess decoding, balance between ingredients, unwanted source resemblance and register. A valid blend can fail because one ingredient disappears perceptually; a valid reversal can suggest undoing useful progress. Repair the rule or input choice, not just the explanation.
8. Pause after a useful contrast or two fruitless source routes. Offer another passage/genre, a different pattern, refinement, shortlist or stopping. Carry parked cards forward. User preference changes the active constraint; enthusiasm alone never authorizes saving or export.

## Actual source observations and limits

L01: Lewis Carroll, *Through the Looking-Glass*, Project Gutenberg Millennium Fulcrum Edition 1.7, chapter VI, exchange explaining `slithy` and `mimsy`. A character explicitly connects paired meanings; this supports an observed semantic-compression pattern. Letter segmentation is the agent's analytical reconstruction, not a surviving draft or an author interview. An illustrative parse `sli[my] + [li]th[e] + y → slithy` includes added `y`; that exact splice is not asserted by the passage.

L02: Shakespeare, *Macbeth*, Folger text, 1.5, FTLN 0379 and surrounding speech. `unsex` visibly places `un-` before a base in a requested transformation. A transferable reversal/removal card is an editorial analysis. This occurrence does not establish Shakespeare coined the word. The violent context is a negative register constraint, not a desirable brand attribute.

Both pages were opened on 2026-11-10. Sources establish the observed language and context, not this library's naming procedure or commercial results. If the work is inaccessible, record that limit and offer a supplied passage or use a construction skill without a literary attribution. Never pretend an agent-created example is a quotation.
