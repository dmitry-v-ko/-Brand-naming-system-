# Fragment Blending

- id: `fragment-blending`
- family: structural construction
- status: draft
- implementation: complete editorial procedure; independent acceptance pending
- requires: research-optional
- outputs: candidate ledger, method artifact, feedback/state delta

## Mechanism and boundary

Retain a head from one meaningful word and a tail from another, deleting material from both. Record cuts and lost recognition. A coincidental sound join is allowed but genuine suffix-prefix overlap belongs to `overlap-telescoping`; meaning-free prebuilt chunks belong to `syllable-recombination`.

## Use, avoid and inputs

Use when two concepts matter but intact compounds feel long. Avoid when users must immediately recover both meanings, when specialist roots are unverified, or when arbitrary cut points leave neither concept recognizable.

Required: two semantic banks and which concept should dominate. Default: 3 × 3 ingredients, heads/tails at syllable or recognizable letter boundaries, two to four intended spoken syllables. Optional: forbidden fragments and maximum spelling length.

## Procedure

1. Choose three heads and three tails from different concept banks. For each source mark two possible cuts and what semantic cue survives.
2. Pair short-head/long-tail and long-head/short-tail variants. Write source, retained segment and deleted segment before accepting a result.
3. Discard forms with no pronounceable vowel per intended syllable or three-consonant boundary clusters unless the supplied audience tolerates them.
4. Compare six survivors in three clusters: first concept dominant, balanced, second concept dominant. Do not claim semantic recognition was tested.
5. Inspect the join aloud with a pronunciation proposal. If a repair adds a sound, record that addition as a phonetic step; do not hide it in the cut map.

## Selection and recovery

Evaluate semantic cue retention, join ease and different balances, rather than a single subjective score. If the result resembles an unrelated everyday word with an unwanted sense, change one cut first. After two unrecognizable repairs, restore a longer source portion or switch to intact compounding. Stop when six forms repeat the same cut and ending; expand the ingredient bank.

## Research and evidence

Research is optional for brief-supplied words. Verify technical senses with a reference entry; dictionary guesses are not roots. Search two explicit source concepts separately rather than searching an invented output and treating unrelated hits as etymology. If sources fail, retain supplied concepts with evidence not applicable or unverified leads without asserted meanings.

## Candidate record and interaction

Use the [session protocol](../../docs/SESSION_PROTOCOL.md) and [source contract](../../docs/SOURCES_AND_RESEARCH.md). Every internal candidate record has stable branch/candidate IDs; English spelling; proposed pronunciation/stress where uncertain; exact lineage; separate `form` and `evidence`; source IDs or `none`; useful association and main drawback. Ordinary responses show 2–3 contrasts with a brief reason, relevant reading/risk and terse evidence status; IDs and detailed tables stay in the exploration log or session-owned research notes unless requested. Batch/grid sizes are private defaults, not user-facing quotas. Do not put real sessions in this library.

For a sufficiently specified brief, proceed with two or three useful contrasts and a recommendation; no separate preview gate is required. Expand menus when requested or real uncertainty changes direction. Record inferred choices as agent judgments, never as user delegation. Ask which property to preserve, not merely which name is “best.” After feedback record the actual words, changed constraints, rejected/parked paths and next options. Praise does not save a name. Add only explicit selections; repeated selection updates the same candidate. A six-name batch is a pause for navigation, never an automatic export.

## Ordered combinations

`lexical-research` → `fragment-blending` preserves sourced cuts. `fragment-blending` → `suffix-attachment` adds a declared ending after the blend. Switch to `overlap-telescoping` only when an actual shared sequence can be demonstrated.
