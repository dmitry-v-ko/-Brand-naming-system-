# Suffix Attachment

- id: `suffix-attachment`
- family: structural construction
- status: draft
- implementation: complete editorial procedure; independent acceptance pending
- requires: research-optional
- outputs: candidate ledger, method artifact, feedback/state delta

## Mechanism and boundary

Add a declared ending to an unchanged stem, comparing role and cadence. Distinguish a semantic ending (doer, place) from an invented ending. Unlike `prefix-attachment`, the entry sound remains fixed; unlike free `phonetic-invention`, the semantic stem survives intact.

## Use, avoid and inputs

Use when a recognizable stem should feel like a tool, community, place or new entity. Avoid indiscriminate -ify/-ly style decoration or when an ending changes the stem so much that its meaning becomes an unsupported root claim.

Required: 2–3 stems, intended role, desired stress. Default: compare `-er` (proposed doer reading), `-ery` (proposed place/activity reading), and invented `-ora` (cadence only). Optional: stem length, sober/playful tone; endings are working associations, not guaranteed interpretations.

## Procedure

1. Keep exact stems in rows and three endings in columns. State intended function and whether it is meaningful or purely sonic.
2. Attach literally; record every added letter. Do not drop a final e silently. Show conventional spelling adaptation as an extra step if requested.
3. Assign proposed stress to each result and inspect double letters and vowel collisions. Compare one semantic and one invented ending on the same stem.
4. Reject inappropriate agent/person readings for an impersonal product, or long ornamental tails for a concise brief.
5. Show three to six distinct stem/role combinations. Expand only the chosen role or sound family after feedback.

## Selection and recovery

Assess retained stem, implied role, rhythm and spelling friction. If a good stem plus ending is awkward, change ending first; changing both obscures learning. If all endings feel cosmetic, return to intact stems and consider `intact-word-compounding`. Do not assert established derivational productivity without a source.

## Research and evidence

For fixture stems, evidence not applicable. To claim a specific historical suffix meaning, query that ending in a dictionary and inspect the sense. If inaccessible, call it an intended association or invented ending. Technical or foreign stems require `lexical-research` or `cross-language-mining`; no invented Latin authority.

## Candidate record and interaction

Every displayed batch has stable branch/candidate IDs; English spelling; proposed pronunciation/stress where uncertain; exact lineage; separate `form` and `evidence`; source IDs or `none`; useful association and main drawback. Show two or three contrasting previews before expanding, unless the user already delegated or specified the operation. Ask which property to preserve, not merely which name is “best.” After feedback record the actual words, changed constraints, rejected/parked paths and next options. Praise does not save a name. Add only explicit selections; repeated selection updates the same candidate. A six-name batch is a pause for navigation, never an automatic export.

## Ordered combinations

`clipping` → `suffix-attachment`: clip the source with its deletion map, then add ending. `cross-language-mining` → `suffix-attachment`: preserve source sense, adaptation and ending separately. `suffix-attachment` → `phonetic-invention` changes sounds in a new branch with an explicit extra operation.
