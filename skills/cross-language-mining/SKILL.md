# Cross-Language Lexical Prospecting

## Purpose
Locate real short expressive roots across languages, then adapt them to fluent English candidate names while preserving transparent provenance.

## When to use
Choose this when its mechanism yields a meaningfully different creative search direction from the branches explored so far. Avoid when the user has explicitly rejected the required sound, imagery or lexical source style.

## Inputs
Use the current brand brief: offer, target audience, desired character, exclusions and user taste. Infer provisional constraints when not provided. User can communicate in any language; names must be in English-compatible Latin script.

## Procedure
1. Define 6–10 semantic motives from the brief (e.g. momentum, attunement, threshold, glow).
2. Offer 5–8 language-family exploration routes (Germanic, Romance, Slavic, Austronesian, Japonic, etc.) and explain their likely sound palettes. Do not assert unsupported cultural characterizations.
3. Consult reliable dictionaries for attested translations, pronunciation and semantic range. Collect 25–40 candidates spanning multiple unrelated languages.
4. Label verification level; record original script, romanization, language, meaning, dictionary source, and pronunciation where available.
5. Screen potential taboo, sacred/restricted meanings and major ambiguity; do not claim universal cross-language suitability.
6. Produce sets of direct loans, lightly anglicized adaptations and new blends; explain every transformation. Aim for 10–15 distinct options.
7. Let the user choose favorite phonetic families; then deepen or move to other language groups.

## Research plan and provenance
Use reputable bilingual or monolingual dictionaries, Wiktionary with corroboration where practical, and scholarly language resources. Prefer entries over machine-translated word lists. Search `site:... dictionary <concept> <language>` and verify definitions. When sources are inaccessible, clearly flag the word as unverified.

## Interaction and navigation
Offer preview directions before conducting costly deep research. After generating a cluster, ask whether to (a) dig deeper into it, (b) explore another source territory, (c) combine this with another method, (d) return to the method menu or (e) shortlist selected candidates. The user decides when to stop.

## Output contract
For each shortlisted-for-discussion suggestion include: `candidate`, `creative tone`, `derivation`, `lexical status` (`verified attested`, `unverified attested`, `adapted`, `invented`), `source reference if research-derived`, and `spoken/pronunciation note when helpful`. Keep claims proportionate to source evidence.

## Example (synthetic; not research-verified)
Synthetic illustration: turning an actually verified root into a newly invented English-friendly ending. Do not assign invented etymology to `Soreva` (synthetic name example).

## Failure modes
- Do not claim that a source, dictionary entry or etymology was checked without actually checking it.
- Do not label a word as original, legally available or trademark-safe.
- Avoid presenting dozens of orthographic variants as conceptually diverse work.
- Keep the user's rejected directions visible in the session log, and do not silently reintroduce them.
- For culturally located vocabulary, preserve nuance and flag likely sensitive material.
