# Cross Language Mining

- id: `cross-language-mining`
- family: cross-language source discovery

- implementation: complete editorial procedure; independent acceptance pending
- requires: research-required for foreign senses
- outputs: candidate ledger, method artifact, feedback/state delta

## Mechanism and boundary

Search a concept in another language, verify a precise sense, then compare direct form with explicit English-facing adaptations. It is not free root invention or historical etymological tracing. Record original script, sense, transliteration where applicable and adaptation separately.

## Use, avoid and inputs

Use when a concept gains expressive range through another language and the audience can tolerate explanation. Avoid when a language is chosen for a stereotyped personality, when cultural context is unknown, or when verified foreign meaning is required but access is absent.

Required: concept cluster and English-facing pronunciation constraints. Default: two languages with accessible reference dictionaries, one precise sense per term, no invented ancient-root claims. Optional: market languages, diacritics policy, native review availability. Native review is an unresolved task unless actually performed.

## Procedure

1. Expand the target concept into 3 related senses; choose two research languages for source access, not national stereotypes. Translation tools provide leads only.
2. Search concept + language dictionary. Open the exact headword; distinguish homonyms and numbered senses. Capture original spelling and source gloss before generating names.
3. Build a term/sense/adaptation ledger. Separate direct source form, script transliteration, accent removal, spelling change and additional construction.
4. Compare one direct form, one minimally adapted form and one declared hybrid. Propose English pronunciation without presenting it as authentic native pronunciation.
5. Review semantic range and uncertain cultural associations. Return 3–6 forms from supported ingredients; if only one term is verified, show a small honest batch rather than padding with guessed roots.

## Selection and recovery

Assess sense fidelity, visible adaptation steps, reading friction and whether the story works without exoticism. If a homonym points somewhere unwanted, preserve the sense distinction but assess listener risk. If adaptation erases the concept, retain a longer source or change term. After two failed dictionaries, park the language and offer a verifiable language or unverified branch.

## Ordered combinations

`cross-language-mining` → `clipping` cuts a sourced term with exact deletions. `cross-language-mining` → `suffix-attachment` retains a declared ending and labels its function. `cross-language-mining` → `intact-word-compounding` adds a supplied English word. Do not describe these constructed outputs as foreign dictionary words.
