# Lexical Research

- id: `lexical-research`

- outputs: candidate ledger, method artifact, feedback/state delta

## Mechanism and boundary

Retrieve and select real vocabulary through senses, related entry links and a relevance ledger. Direct unchanged words can become candidates. The distinctive decision is definition-led discovery and selection, not a new spelling operator or merely changing the source world.

## Use, avoid and inputs

Use for evocative real words, technical/archaic language and credible source stories. Avoid when no access exists but the user requires verified findings, when rare words need lengthy explanations, or when the available terms contradict the brand promise.

Required: one desired property plus at least one exclusion. Infer two distant universes and offer 6–12 if user wants a full menu. Default search budget: 3 queries, 2 opened useful entries per chosen direction, 6–10 ingredient rows. Optional: era, register, language, rarity and audience familiarity.

## Procedure

1. Translate the brief into searchable properties rather than category keywords: “quiet transformation,” “connection without rigidity.” Propose vivid universe previews, labeled unverified until opened.
2. Select one world by user choice or delegated assumption. Search an official/reference glossary using property + glossary and exact candidate terms. Open entries and capture the precise sense/locator.
3. Build a ledger: exact word, meaning, related-entry link, relevance, misleading implication, evidence. Follow at most two useful links; avoid uncontrolled thesaurus drift.
4. Separate direct-name options from adaptation ingredients. For direct use retain exact spelling; for a changed form invoke a separate named skill.
5. Ordinarily present 2–3 contrasting terms across useful functional, sensory or abstract directions, including one near miss and why it failed. Stop after two failed research routes or when additional entries repeat the same property.

## Selection and recovery

Select by supported sense, useful relevance with little explanation, English-facing decoding and balanced implications. Rare is not inherently distinctive or good. If a source is insufficient, record it, switch to another reference and inspect the actual entry. If meaning conflicts, reject or move to a brief that honestly fits; never rewrite the definition to rescue a name.

## Candidate record and interaction

Use the [session protocol](../../docs/SESSION_PROTOCOL.md) and [source contract](../../docs/SOURCES_AND_RESEARCH.md). Every internal candidate record has stable branch/candidate IDs; English spelling; proposed pronunciation/stress where uncertain; exact lineage; separate `form` and `evidence`; source IDs or `none`; useful association and main drawback. Ordinary responses show 2–3 contrasts with a brief reason, relevant reading/risk and terse evidence status; IDs and detailed tables stay in the exploration log or session-owned research notes unless requested. Batch/grid sizes are private defaults, not user-facing quotas. Do not put real sessions in this library.

For a sufficiently specified brief, proceed with two or three useful contrasts and a recommendation; no separate preview gate is required. Expand menus when requested or real uncertainty changes direction. Record inferred choices as agent judgments, never as user delegation. Ask which property to preserve, not merely which name is “best.” After feedback record the actual words, changed constraints, rejected/parked paths and next options. Praise does not save a name. Add only explicit selections; repeated selection updates the same candidate. A six-name batch is a pause for navigation, never an automatic export.

## Ordered combinations

`lexical-research` → `metaphor-mining` maps an observed property. `lexical-research` → `clipping` retains an exact deletion map. `lexical-research` → `intact-word-compounding` keeps ingredient spelling intact. Direct use is not counted as another construction method.
